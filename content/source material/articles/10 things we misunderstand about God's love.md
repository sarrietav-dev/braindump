# Misconceptions About God’s Love

These are my notes on the article: [10 Things We Get Wrong About the Love of God | Crossway Articles](https://www.crossway.org/articles/10-things-we-get-wrong-about-the-love-of-god)

> [!warning] Disclaimer
> At the time of writing, I don’t fully understand the Calvinism vs. Arminianism debate.
>
> What I roughly understand is that Calvinists believe God chooses who is saved, while Arminians believe humans can freely choose to believe and be saved, or not.
>
> I mention this because whenever I see the word “elect” (which appears about 12 times in the article), it reminds me of Calvinism.
>
> I am neither for nor against this theology until I understand it better.

1. **God doesn’t owe us His love.** He is sovereign and can give or withhold His love freely. It seems there are two kinds of love, one of which is the electing love (also called salvific love), which is not given to “sinners deserving hell.”

2. **His love is not monolithic.** His love can vary depending on His purpose, His goodwill, and other reasons. For example, the way He shows kindness, mercy, and patience to all (common grace) contrasts with how He redeems, regenerates, and grants eternal life to those who trust in Him (special grace). According to D. A. Carson, the Bible speaks of God’s love in five ways:
   1. The Father’s love for the Son.
   2. Love for His creation (everything He made was “good”).
   3. Love for the fallen world.
   4. Love for those He chooses (Israel, the church, individuals).
   5. Conditional love (for those who remain in Him, keep His commandments, and fear Him).

3. **His love doesn’t mean everyone will be saved.** That is reserved only for the elect. If someone goes to hell, it is not an act of injustice, but of perfect justice.

4. **Christ’s death doesn’t guarantee God’s love.** Jesus died because God loves us, not the other way around. If it were the other way, it would mean Christ had to die for us to receive a small dose of love from a reluctant and unwilling God. [John 3:16](https://www.esv.org/verses/John%203%3A16/)

5. **God doesn’t love us because He knew we would love Him.** There is nothing we can be or do to deserve His love. In fact, everything He did was because He loved us first. [Romans 8:29](https://www.esv.org/verses/Rom.%208%3A29/)

6. **He doesn’t love us because humanity fell into sin.** His love is eternal. “He chose us before the foundation of the world” ([Ephesians 1:4–5](https://www.esv.org/verses/Eph.%201%3A4%E2%80%935/)).

7. **His love will never be withdrawn or revoked.** “Who shall separate us from the love of Christ? Tribulation, distress, persecution, famine, nakedness, danger, or sword?” ([Romans 8:35](https://www.esv.org/verses/Rom.%208%3A35/))

8. **His love doesn’t mean He will always spare us from suffering or never discipline us.** Hard times, far from being a sign of God’s rejection, are proof of His paternal love for us. “My son, do not regard lightly the discipline of the Lord, nor be weary when reproved by Him. For the Lord disciplines the one He loves, and chastises every son whom He receives.” ([Hebrews 12:5–6](https://www.esv.org/verses/Heb.%2012%3A5%E2%80%936/))

9. **God’s love is not only for saving sinners.** It also leads us to mutual love, obedience, and security in Christ. ([1 John 4:12](https://www.esv.org/verses/1%20John%204%3A12/), [1 John 4:7–11](https://www.esv.org/verses/1%20John%404%3A7%E2%80%9311/))

10. **God’s love for Himself is not selfish.** His primary goal is to secure praise, honor, and glory for Himself. But that doesn’t mean He doesn’t love us. Every joy leads to worship of something, so there is nothing better than delighting in the most excellent Being in the universe, whose greatest joy is that we love Him. Therefore, for God, seeking His glory through our worship is the most loving act He can do for us.
