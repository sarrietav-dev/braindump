# The Big Puzzle Box Top

We can imagine what gives unity to the diversity of things in life as the top of a puzzle box. This shows us the complete picture when all the pieces fit together.

World religions seek to be that box top, offering the existence of God as the answer. This makes sense because many of the great philosophical questions find their answer in God:

- Who are we?
- Why are we here?
- Where are we going?
- How should we live?
- Where did we come from?

# Religious Worldviews

The main religious worldviews can be summarized in three:

- **Theism**: Believes in a personal God who created everything, but who is distinct from His creation. Example: a painter (God) and his painting (creation).
- **Pantheism**: God is impersonal and is everything that exists: the universe, the Earth, the trees, the stars, you, me… everything is God. Example: God is the painting itself.
- **Atheism**: Does not believe in any God. Only the painting exists and no one painted it; it has always been there.

There are also **agnostics**, who are not sure or simply do not want to take a position.

# All Worldviews Require Faith

In reality, all worldviews require a degree of faith, since we are limited beings and do not possess absolute knowledge to prove with certainty the existence of God. We live in a world of probabilities, so any claim about God could be false.

Each point of view must present evidence to support its beliefs. The more evidence there is, the less faith is needed to believe. And by comparing the evidence for and against the existence of God, we can conclude that it takes more faith to be an atheist than to be a Christian. (😱)

# I Refuse to Believe!

There are people who, no matter how much evidence you give them, simply will not want to believe!

Here emotional and willful factors come into play. They do not want to believe because accepting the truth would mean changing their lifestyle, thoughts, friendships, values, priorities, and giving up control. They know that Christianity is not just about receiving forgiveness, but also about denying oneself every day.

On the other hand, there are also Christians who believe without questioning because they **want** to believe. But the same happens with atheists: many adopt their position without evaluating the evidence, taking a great leap of faith in their atheism because they **want** it to be true. There are also **agnostics**, who prefer to remain neutral, justifying it with the idea of being “open-minded.” However, there is a big difference between having an open mind and having an empty mind.

True open-mindedness means recognizing the truth when we find it. If there is enough evidence, agnostics are not taking a neutral position, but avoiding taking a stand, which is equivalent to an empty mind.

# Truth

## We Ignore Religious and Moral Truth Because It Doesn't Suit Us

We demand to be told the truth in many areas of our lives:
- In our close relationships.
- In the news.
- In the books we read.
- In medical appointments.
- In our finances.

But why don't we demand the truth in matters of religion or morality?

The rejection of this truth is not due to lack of evidence, but to lack of will. We do not want to be held accountable for our actions under any moral or religious standard.

Therefore, we adopt ideas that relativize truth, such as:

- “Truth is relative and there are no absolutes.”
- “It's true for you, but not for me.”
- “Truth does not exist.”
- “You shouldn't judge.”

## What is Truth?

**Truth** is *to say things as they are* or *to describe the real state of things*.

### **Characteristics of Truth**

- **Truth is absolute**, even when it seems relative.
  - Example: “I felt very cold one night in Bogotá” is a subjective experience, but the statement that cold was felt is still objective and true.

- **Truth is transcultural**: It is true for everyone, at any time and place.

- **Truth is discovered, not invented**:
  - Example: Gravity existed before Newton explained it.

- **Truth is exclusive**:
  - “Everything is true” excludes its opposite: “Everything is false.”
  - Any truth excludes the contrary, including religious truths.

- **Truth does not change, but our beliefs about it do**:
  - It was once believed that the Earth was flat, but the reality is that it has always been round.

- **Beliefs do not change the truth**:
  - Someone may sincerely believe the Earth is flat, but that does not make it flat, it only makes them sincerely wrong.

- **A person's attitude does not affect the truth**:
  - A truth said arrogantly does not make it false.
  - A lie said humbly does not make it true.

- **There can be opposing beliefs, but not opposing truths**.

## The Roadrunner Tactic

The **Roadrunner Tactic** is a method to identify statements that seem true but contradict themselves. It consists of applying a premise to itself and evaluating whether it holds up or collapses.

> [!note] Note
> This tactic seems very useful but must be difficult to identify and analyze one of these statements.

### **Examples of the Roadrunner Tactic**

- **“Truth does not exist”** → Is that statement true?
  - If it is true, then at least one truth exists.
  - If it is false, the statement self-destructs.

- **“All truth is relative”** → Is that truth relative?
  - If it is relative, then it could be false.
  - If it is not relative, then there are absolute truths.

- **“There are no absolute truths”** → Are you absolutely sure?
  - If someone is absolutely sure, then absolute truths exist.
  - If not, they admit the possibility of absolute truths.

- **“It's true for you, but not for me”** → Is that statement true for everyone?
  - If it is true for everyone, then it is an absolute truth.
  - If not, it is just an opinion.
- **“Truth cannot be known”** → How do you know that's true?
  - It is self-evident…
- **“I'm a skeptic”** → Should you doubt your skepticism?
  - If you do not doubt your skepticism, then you are not a skeptic.

> [!info]- Why is it called the Roadrunner Tactic?
> The name comes from the cartoon **Roadrunner and Coyote**. In the story, the coyote chases the roadrunner, but the roadrunner, cleverly, stops right at the edge of a cliff. The coyote keeps running and, realizing he has no ground under his feet, falls into the void.
>
> The same happens with these contradictory statements: when examined by their own criteria, they are revealed as unsustainable and “fall into the void.”

## Can All Religions Be True?

No. Although on the surface some religions share certain beliefs, at their core they hold opposite and contradictory principles. Some examples:

- **Final destiny of humanity**:
  - Christians believe that those who do not accept Christ as their savior face eternal condemnation.
  - Muslims believe the same about those who do not follow Islam.
  - Hindus believe in an eternal cycle of reincarnation based on the actions of each life.

- **Nature of God**:
  - Jews, Christians, and Muslims believe in a theistic God, though with differences among them.
  - Hindus believe in pantheism, where everything is part of an impersonal divinity.

- **Nature of evil**:
  - Many Hindus consider evil to be an illusion.
  - Jews, Christians, and Muslims believe evil is real.

- **Salvation**:
  - Christians believe salvation is given by grace.
  - Other religions hold that salvation depends on good works.

Since these beliefs are **mutually contradictory**, not all can be true. If one is true, the rest must be false.

## Problems with the Religion of Pluralism

Religious pluralism holds that all religious beliefs are true in some sense. However, this idea presents several contradictions:

- If different religious beliefs are exclusive to each other, then it makes no sense to claim that all are true at the same time.
- The statement **“You can't question other religious beliefs”** is itself an exclusive and intolerant belief, as it implies that those who question beliefs are wrong.
- Furthermore, this statement is an **absolute moral belief**.
  - Why would it be wrong to question religious beliefs?
  - If it's just an opinion, why is it imposed on others?

- **Christians have the responsibility to question religious beliefs.**
  - If pluralism holds that all beliefs must be tolerated, then it must also tolerate the Christian belief that truth must be defended.
  - However, in practice, many pluralists do not tolerate the Christian stance, which makes them inconsistent with their own claim of universal tolerance.

- **The command “Do not judge” in Matthew 7:1-5 has been misinterpreted.**
  - Saying *“Do not judge”* is itself a judgment.
  - The question is not whether we should judge, but whether we are making the right judgments.

- **Are pluralists willing to tolerate the beliefs of extremists?**
  - For example, would they tolerate the beliefs of Muslim terrorists who hold that non-Muslims must be eliminated?
  - Obviously, we hope not.

We must **respect the right** of people to believe what they wish, but **it would be unloving to tell them that all beliefs are true**.

If Christianity is true, claiming that all religions are equally valid would lead people to eternal condemnation.
