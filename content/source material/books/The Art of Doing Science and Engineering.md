# Orientation

> Merely copying the past will not be enough if you aspire to future greatness.

Usually a painter goes to study under a master so he can get feedback from his master's style, and then develop his own style.

> Education is what, when, and why to do things.
> Training is how to do it.

Knowledge doubles every 17 years, and knowledge gets obsolete every 15 years. Hence why is important to adapt to learn new concepts. The key is to learn the fundamentals because most innovations are derived from them. Most of the chapter focuses on this.

> You have to actively master on your own the many emerging fields as they arise.

> The past was once the future and the future will become the past.

The future is rather uncertain, but we must try to predict it. As the problem of the drunken sailor: his random, independent steps will lead him nowhere; but if he sees a pretty girl, at least his steps won't be so random anymore, because they will lead him to the girl... somewhat.
As well, having a vision is important if you want to go far, because:

> In a sense, the main difference between those who go far and those who do not is some people have a vision and the others do not and therefore can only react to the current events as they happen.

And

> Getting anywhere is better than drifting.

To formulate the vision, answer these questions:
- What is possible? (related to *science*)
- What is likely to happen? (related to *engineering*)
- What is desirable to have happen? (related to *morals*)

> The true gain is in the struggle and not in the achievement — a life without a struggle to make yourself excellent is hardly a life worth living
