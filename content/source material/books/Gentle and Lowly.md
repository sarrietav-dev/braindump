Jesus described His own heart: gentle and lowly.

In both the Old and New Testaments, the heart is the center of our entire being. It is our life and **who we are**.

Gentle means He is not harsh or easily angered. He understands our position and will always welcome us with open arms.

Lowly means He is accessible; there are no prerequisites or obstacles to approach Him. All that is needed is a willingness to draw near.

What He desires is for us to find rest in Him, to come out of the storm.

This description applies only to those who repent of their sins. This heart never changes, regardless of our insecurities, doubts, fears, or failures. He cannot be anything other than gentle and lowly, because that is His very nature!

The Christian life is full of effort and labor, and Christ does not seek to give us physical rest, but rest for our souls.

We only need to approach that heart to experience true Christian life, which allows us to leave a trail and fragrance of Jesus’ kindness wherever we go.

We often fail to think of Jesus as kind, patient, and approachable. We project human assumptions onto Him, such as a rich person looking down on the poor or the beautiful despising the ugly. We imagine that when Jesus tries to reach us, He does so as a child touching a slug: with disgust, instantly pulling back.

That is why reading the Bible is important: passages like this deconstruct preconceived ideas and nourish us with the infinite perfections of God.
