Trials serve to reveal the legitimacy or illegitimacy of our faith. They determine whether a person trusts and loves God in difficult times or not. This is the main theme James seeks to address in his epistle.

Trials cannot destroy faith; they can only prove it.

# Reasons Why God Sends Trials

- To separate us from the world.
- To reveal what we truly love.
- To focus our minds on the eternal.
- To show us the value of blessings and God's grace.
- To enable us to support others in their own trials.
- To equip us for greater works.
- To discipline us for our sin.

True faith will persevere to the end of the trial.

# How to Persevere

During a trial, a true Christian must have:

## A Joyful Attitude

Because trials strengthen us, bring us closer to God, and don't destroy our faith, but make us more useful.

> [!quote]
> **2** Count it all joy, my brothers, when you meet trials of various kinds.
>
> — James 1:2 (ESV)

## An Understanding Mind

Knowing that trials produce endurance.

> [!quote]
> **3** for you know that the testing of your faith produces steadfastness.
>
> — James 1:3 (ESV)

## A Submissive Will

Allow the trial to bring endurance and let the trial produce perfection. Let it accomplish what God intends.

> [!quote]
> **4** And let steadfastness have its full effect, that you may be perfect and complete, lacking in nothing.
>
> — James 1:4 (ESV)

# Wisdom

We need wisdom to go through a trial because, in the midst of it, we might not know how to remain joyful, we might not understand why it's happening, or we might resist being shaped by it.

Ask God for wisdom beyond human faculties.

> [!quote]
> **5** If any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask God, who gives generously to all without reproach, and it will be given him.
>
> — James 1:5 (ESV)

Wisdom is the understanding necessary to live a life for God's glory—fearing and obeying Him.

Don't lean on your own understanding.

It's not philosophical speculation, but God's understanding and perspective, which is "*pure, then peaceable, gentle, open to reason, full of mercy and good fruits, impartial and sincere*" (James 3:17).

See Job 28:12-23.

The pursuit of wisdom is **mandatory** in a trial because it brings us closer to God (*ask God*).

And He will give it to us generously, freely, abundantly, and without conditions.
