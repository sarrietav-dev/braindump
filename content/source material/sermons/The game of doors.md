We begin life regretting what we did, but we finish it regretting what we didn't do.

We don't have to spiritualize every decision we make in life.

> [!quote] > [8] “I know your works. Behold, I have set before you an open door, which no one is able to shut…” (ESV)
>
> — Revelation 3:8

We often try to make God like an oracle or horoscope so that He can tell us what to do — often thinking about our own desires.

The real reason is that we want to avoid making important choices in life. And if that decision goes wrong, instead of taking responsibility, we can blame God. We don't want to deal with the anxiety of deciding.

Decision-making shapes character! We have to make decisions to grow, even if they are mistakes!

God's main will is about the person we become, not the specific path we take to get there.

We can become paralyzed by the 'What ifs?' which can lead to depression, anxiety, and ungratefulness.

If easiness is the criteria for what we consider to be faith, then when difficult times come, we will struggle with doubt. An open door doesn't mean an easy life.

Spiritual maturity is the ability to stand up to problems without losing peace.

God rarely opens the biggest doors. In the Bible we have examples like Moses not entering the promised land or David being denied the chance to build the temple.

One of God's greatest gifts is unanswered prayers.
