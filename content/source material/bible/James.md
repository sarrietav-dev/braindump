# James 1

## James 1:2-18

We should have joy in trials because they allow us to develop steadfastness, which allows God to perfect us.

We should ask God for wisdom with faith, because he who doubts is like "a wave driven by the wind, unstable in all his ways."

We are like the flower of the field, which blooms with all its splendor, but in a moment it withers and its beauty perishes.

And those who remain faithful in trials will be blessed and receive the crown of life.

## James 1:19-27

It is not enough to hear God's word, we must obey it! Sometimes we are too quick to share our opinions and we don't let His word penetrate from our mind to our heart.

# James 2

## James 2:1-6

The passage seems to address someone who gives preference to the rich (perhaps to gain more influence and power) and sets aside the poor. This goes against God's impartial nature.

## James 2:7-13

James says that if you show partiality, you commit sin against the commandment "You shall love your neighbor as yourself," and to support this he mentions that if someone does not fulfill a single commandment, they become guilty of the whole law.

## James 2:14-26

In this difficult passage, James seems to contradict Paul by stating that we are not justified by works. But actually, he is saying that works are the evidence of faith. True faith _produces_ works. He also gives examples of Abraham's works when he was about to sacrifice his son, and of Rahab when she trusted God and helped the Hebrew spies escape from Jericho.

According to the **ESV Study Bible**, when James uses the word **justify**, what he means is that _works demonstrate that someone has been justified_.

According to the **ESV Men's Devotional Bible**, we must distinguish the way James uses the word "faith." Sometimes he refers to _true, saving, and living faith_; other times he refers to _false, useless, and dead faith_.
